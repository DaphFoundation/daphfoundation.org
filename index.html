We face a triple crisis

People across the country face compounding challenges that demand urgent, coordinated action from our communities.

⚕️

Health Inequity

Millions lack access to adequate healthcare, especially people with disabilities and low-income families living in underserved communities.

🏘️

Housing Insecurity

Rising costs and systemic barriers push vulnerable populations out of safe, accessible housing — threatening stability and dignity.

🤝

Social Isolation

Disconnection and despair weaken civic life. People feel unheard, unseen, and left behind by institutions that should serve them.

About Us

Building power for those left behind

DAPH builds the power of people with disabilities and under-resourced communities in urban, rural, and suburban areas to win change through issue fights and elections. We are a coalition of grassroots organizers united in the work of building a bigger "we."

Since our founding, we have won material improvements for millions — including expanded healthcare access, accessible housing protections, and stronger community mental health programs.

A Crisis You Can Help End

They show up to school.
Will you show up for them?

These children wake up before sunrise and walk to school on empty stomachs. Their parents love them deeply — but poverty has left them without options. There is no money for school fees. No money for books. Sometimes, no money for food. Yet still, these children come. They sit on broken chairs or bare floors, hungry and hopeful. They haven't given up. We can't give up on them.

A classroom without chairs.
A child without a meal.
This is real.

Deep in an impoverished community, hundreds of children gather under makeshift roofs — walls of bamboo and thatch, floors of red earth. They have uniforms. They have the will. What they don't have is enough.

Their parents are not absent — they are exhausted. Every coin that comes in must first go to rent, to water, to survival. School fees go unpaid. Textbooks go unbought. Children go hungry through the school day, struggling to concentrate on lessons their minds are too weak to hold.

Some carry their belongings in plastic bags because they cannot afford a backpack. Some share a single chair. Some come barefoot. They come anyway. Because they believe education is their way out — if only someone would open the door.

That someone is you. A donation of any size funds a school term, buys supplies, provides a meal, and tells a child: you are worth investing in.
